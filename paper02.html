与整合护理团队共同设计：建立信息需求清单

Co-design with Integrated Care Teams: Establishing Information Needs

P.J. White, Brian P. Casey, Olga Cleary, Emer Finn, Kate O'Connor, Neville Coen International Journal of Integrated Care2023; 23(4): 7, 1-14 DOI: 10.5334/ijic.7618
Impact Factor: 2.6 Q1 (SCImago) JCR Q2 · Rank #66/174 in Health Care Sciences & Services

Abstract · 摘要
English

Introduction: Co-design has been cited as playing a major role in the future of effective integrated care, however, there is a lack of reporting and reflection on the methods used. Information sharing is fundamental when working in integrated care, however sharing across professions, service settings and localities can be complex. Through co-design, we seek to establish a shared understanding of information needs within a newly formed integrated care team. In doing so we aim to inform future practice in the understanding of co-design.

Description: Co-design Workshop 1 (N = 24 participants, plus 6 facilitators), collected 'Current Position' understanding of service information needs. Co-design Workshop 2 (N = 18 participants, plus 6 facilitators) sought a 'Future Position' understanding, identifying solutions and next steps for establishing information-need solutions. Reflection on the co-design process was conducted to inform future co-design practices.

Conclusion: Identified was a wide range of future service information needs under the themes of Culture Building, Health System Needs, and Processes. We conclude with 4 key learning points on co-designing: (1) Ensure simplicity in format. (2) Interdisciplinary co-design and co-facilitation of workshops are beneficial. (3) Planning and preparation are key. (4) Co-designing can enhance communication for service improvement.

中文

引言：Co-design被认为在未来有效整合护理中将发挥重要作用，然而目前关于所用方法的报告与反思极为匮乏。信息共享是整合护理工作的基础，但跨专业、跨服务设置和地域的共享往往十分复杂。通过co-design，我们旨在在一个新成立的整合护理团队内建立对信息需求的共同理解。以此，我们希望为未来理解co-design实践提供参考。

描述：第一次Co-design工作坊（24名参与者，外加6名引导者）收集了服务信息需求的"当前位置"理解。第二次Co-design工作坊（18名参与者，外加6名引导者）寻求"未来位置"理解，识别解决方案并制定建立信息需求解决方案的后续步骤。对co-design过程进行了反思，以赋能未来co-design实践。

结论：在文化建设、医疗系统需求和流程三大主题下识别了广泛的未来服务信息需求。我们总结了4条关于co-design的关键学习点：（1）保持形式简洁；（2）跨学科co-design与工作坊协作引导有益；（3）规划与准备是关键；（4）Co-design能够增强服务改进中的沟通。

Background Context: The Irish Sláintecare Reform · 背景：爱尔兰Sláintecare改革
English

The 'Sláintecare' programme was established in 2017 to transform Irish health and social care services and to create a universal single-tier system. Sláintecare's quadruple aim is to improve patient/service user experience, improve clinician experience, lower costs and achieve better outcomes. In achieving these aims, there is a need to re-orientate the model of care towards integrated and community care.

Improving clinician experience in integrated and community care requires a deep understanding of stakeholder needs for effective service change to optimize healthcare system performance and enhance patient/service user experience and outcomes.

Published in 2014, the 'Community Healthcare Organisations — Report and Recommendations of the Integrated Service Area Review Group' sets out a blueprint for how the Health Service Executive (HSE) primary and social care services will be organized and managed in the community. Community Healthcare Networks (CHNs) are a foundational step in building a better health service.

Community care structures are now established, including 96 Community Healthcare Networks (CHN) aligned to 9 Community Healthcare Organisations. Each CHN operates across an average population of 50,000 with between 4–6 multi-disciplinary care teams working together to deliver the Sláintecare vision of providing the right care, in the right place at the right time.

CHNs will enable healthcare decisions to be made closer to the point of care, and specific to population needs with an emphasis on coordination and integration between community services and between community and acute service.

An integral part of integrated care is that professionals from different disciplines and services work together to provide high-quality care, this requires a high degree of information exchange about and with patients. As CHNs are established, a key challenge is developing mechanisms to support information-driven healthcare within the limitations of clinical and health and social care information systems.

改善整合护理和社区护理中的临床医生体验，需要深入理解利益相关者的需求，以通过有效的服务变革优化医疗系统性能，并提升患者/服务用户体验和结果。

2014年发布的《社区医疗组织——综合服务区域审查组报告与建议》为健康服务管理局（HSE）初级护理和社会护理服务如何在社区中组织和管理提供了蓝图。社区医疗网络（CHNs）是建设更好医疗服务的基础步骤。

CHN将使医疗决策更贴近护理点，并根据人群需求进行定制，强调社区服务之间以及社区与急性服务之间的协调与整合。

The Need · 需求分析
English

Team engagement and information sharing are fundamental aspects of integrated care working. Major efforts have been made to move healthcare services from silos of institutional-type care into community settings. However, despite progress services remain fragmented and in many cases, access to information is limited with data systems and infrastructure underdeveloped and team working weak.

In the Irish health service, Health and Social Care Professionals (HSCPs) are the second largest clinical group representing 25% of the clinical workforce and 14% of the overall health service workforce. To promote integrated working, HSCPs may need to change the way they work, strengthen the delivery of 'end-to-end' care and enable the closing of linkages between primary and secondary care.

For HSCPs to promote integrated working and to improve service user outcomes, effective and appropriate communication is a specific competency. There is a need then for HSCPs to have relevant filtered, focused and accessible information available to support effective patient care directly and across their team.

Efficient information sharing has the potential to mitigate adverse impacts of excessive administrative burden on clinicians in favour of clinical care. Several initiatives are underway to address these challenges in community healthcare nationally, which will require translation into more local integrated care contexts. Establishing an understanding of HSCPs information needs for integrated care work is required in the first instance.

Benefits and Need for Co-Design in Healthcare and Integrated Care · Co-design在医疗与整合护理中的益处与需求
English

Integrated care is adaptive to suit the needs of the service users, therefore there is a need to understand specific local contexts, values and preferences. As we prepare for structural change in our health services, HSCPs responsible for the delivery of community healthcare services in CHNs must contribute to shaping the future of care in our community.

The most successful international integrated care programmes have adopted a bottom-up person-centred approach involving service users and clinicians. Co-design has been identified by the HSE as a crucial methodology to achieve this, with a key reform theme being to: "...work in partnership with service users, to co-design new ways of improving the experience of care, and support staff in the delivery of services."

Co-design is now used as a key method in healthcare reform. According to Ní Shé & Harrison, co-design in healthcare is the process of bringing together service users, clinical and non-clinical staff, and relevant groups to work together to improve services. It involves the equal partnership of individuals who work within the system and individuals who have lived experience of using it. Co-design is intended to be meaningful engagement; occurring across all stages (of project work), and it can range in intensity from passive to high involvement.

When discussing co-design, it is important to note definitions from a design practitioner's viewpoint. For example, Sanders and Stappers describe co-design as the "creativity of designers and people not trained in design working together in the design development process". This definition points to the important role of trained designers to inform the process and ensure that designing is central to the process.

According to Moll et al., the co-design process seeks to "tap into tacit knowledge, creativity and shared meaning of diverse perspectives to co-create a shared vision for improvement".

The distinguishing feature of co-design processes led by designers is the involvement of forms of 'making' in a practice based manner e.g. prototyping. These allow participants to explore, share and test a vision of improvement with others. Roberts et al. state that "...the application of design approaches within healthcare help drive necessary innovation in care delivery models". Furthermore, leveraging the knowledge, experiences, and insights of end-users is proven to achieve impactful innovations, benefiting researchers, practitioners, processes, and research outcomes.

Wolstenholme, Grindell and Dearden state that co-design can lead to healthcare teams exhibiting characteristics that support innovation in the long term. When used to improve healthcare systems, Langley, Wolstenholme and Cooke report that it creates the right conditions for knowledge to be mobilized and activated, helping to make sure that improvements are useful and relevant.

Co-designing is advantageous when working with teams as it leads to more long-term success, more support and enthusiasm for change, and can generate solutions that improve day-to-day experiences.

Co-design methods and processes have been cited as playing a major role in the future of effective integrated care. However, a knowledge gap lies in the underdevelopment of co-design processes in health care and HSCPs involvement in this. This knowledge gap is a result of a lack of reporting and reflection on using methods in the field. Ward et al. state that although co-design has great potential as a methodology for understanding healthcare; there is a gap in the peer-reviewed literature on 'how to do' co-design in practice.

Noting the need for a co-design approach in healthcare, Wolstenholme, Grindell and Dearden state that further exploration of the use of design in service improvement is required. At a national level, there is a need for HSCPs to co-design the planning of services across all aspects of health service delivery.

中文

整合护理具有适应性以满足服务用户的需求，因此需要理解特定的地方情境、价值观和偏好。随着我们为医疗服务结构变革做准备，负责在CHN中提供社区医疗服务的HSCPs必须为塑造社区护理的未来做出贡献。

最成功的国际整合护理项目采用了自下而上的以人为本的方法，涉及服务用户和临床医生。爱尔兰卫生服务局（HSE）已将co-design确定为实现这一目标的关键方法论，一个关键改革主题是："与服务用户合作，共同设计改善护理体验的新方式，并支持员工提供服务"。

Co-design目前已被用作医疗改革的关键方法。根据Ní Shé和Harrison的观点，医疗中的co-design是将服务用户、临床和非临床工作人员以及相关群体聚集在一起共同改善服务的过程。它涉及系统内工作者和具有系统使用经验者的平等伙伴关系。Co-design旨在实现有意义的参与，贯穿所有阶段（项目工作），其强度可以从被动到高度参与不等。

讨论co-design时，重要的是注意设计从业者视角的定义。例如，Sanders和Stappers将co-design描述为"设计者和未受过设计训练的人在设计开发过程中共同发挥创造力"。这一定义指出了训练有素的设计者在赋能过程和确保设计成为过程核心方面的重要作用。

根据Moll等人的观点，co-design过程旨在"挖掘不同视角的隐性知识、创造力和共享意义，以共同创造改进的共同愿景"。

由设计者主导的co-design过程的显著特征是以实践为基础的方式（如原型设计）参与"制作"形式。这些使参与者能够与他人一起探索、分享和测试改进愿景。Roberts等人指出，"……设计方法在医疗中的应用有助于推动护理交付模式中的必要创新"。此外，利用终端用户的知识、经验和见解已被证明能够实现有影响力的创新，使研究人员、从业者、流程和研究成果受益。

Wolstenholme、Grindell和Dearden指出，co-design可以引导医疗团队展现出长期支持创新的特征。当用于改善医疗系统时，Langley、Wolstenholme和Cooke报告称，它创造了知识被调动和激活的正确条件，有助于确保改进是有用且相关的。

Co-design在团队工作中具有优势，因为它能带来更长期的成功、更多的变革支持和热情，并能产生改善日常体验的解决方案。

Co-design方法和过程被认为在未来有效整合护理中将发挥重要作用。然而，知识差距在于医疗保健中co-design过程的发展不足以及HSCPs对此的参与。这一知识差距源于该领域缺乏对所用方法的报告和反思。Ward等人指出，尽管co-design作为理解医疗的方法论具有巨大潜力，但同行评审文献中关于"如何做"co-design的实践仍存在空白。

注意到医疗中对co-design方法的需求，Wolstenholme、Grindell和Dearden指出，需要进一步探索设计在服务改进中的使用。在国家层面，HSCPs需要共同设计医疗服务交付各个方面的服务规划。

Methodology · 方法论
English

To meet the aim and objectives, co-design methods were developed and used over 2 co-design workshops to support service understanding of information needs for clinicians, HSCPs and health service management operational in a newly formed Community Healthcare Network. The identified participants were interdisciplinary, consisting of Occupational Therapy, Physiotherapy, Speech and Language and Dietetics as well as engagement with Public Health Nursing, General Practice, Health and Well-being and Primary Care Management (Workshop 1: N = 24 and Workshop 2: N = 18).

The facilitation team (N = 6) was also interdisciplinary, consisting of two experienced co-design facilitators from Product Design and Design Engineering disciplines, two HSCPs and two healthcare managers, including the CHN manager. The facilitation team designed, planned and prepared the structure and original content for the workshops purposefully to tailor directly to the needs of the HSCPs. The co-design workshops were conducted and facilitated in person in keeping with COVID-19 guidelines.

For co-designing, a design-led qualitative interpretivist approach was taken to engage with stakeholders and a grounded theory approach to analysis. Interpretivist approaches were used to ensure shared understandings and meanings with participants. Due to the inclusive nature of its process, participant-led co-design through a practice-based approach was the core methodology used. This ensured that stakeholders designed from not only their point of view but also shared and drew from the experiences of others, guaranteeing that a deep, empathic, and unbiased understanding is gained.

Reflections on the experiences of co-designing were conducted by and were from the design and facilitation team using the Gibbs reflective cycle. In this, the facilitation team engaged in collaborative reflection sessions where they shared their observations and insights. This occurred after each workshop. Describing and evaluating the positive and negative feelings and thoughts of the experience, analyzing to make sense of the situation and conclusion about what was learned and what you could have done differently for the future.

After consulting with the national HSE Research and Development Office, guidance was given that as this was a service quality improvement initiative with a focus on co-design methodology, it was deemed to fall outside the scope of the HSE RGMS Framework. Quality improvement falls under section 4.1.4 Activities that do not require Research Ethics Committee (REC) approval.

There was no obligation for participation but those who had an interest in service improvement and the capacity/availability to attend were encouraged, no incentives were offered to participate. Participation was encouraged by the Network Management and supported by senior health services management as part of a service improvement approach. Permission to leave their clinical role was granted for the workshop.

Awareness of any potential power dynamics between disciplines and participants was important when facilitating the co-design workshops. This was facilitated by being conscious of equal time and voice of each participant in each group when possible. The design and facilitation team set out the objectives of the workshop in an introductory presentation at both events and highlighted the importance of everyone's contribution across disciplines and the need to ensure there was a focus on understanding the information needs of all representatives to move towards an integrated team approach in the Network.

Participants did not record their names on their ideas during the co-design activities, reinforcing the message that all ideas were equally valued regardless of seniority or discipline. In addition, more junior HSCP representatives as well as discipline managers were purposely recruited to capture diverse information needs thereby ensuring a balanced input from staff grades.

中文

为达成目的和目标，co-design方法在两次co-design工作坊中开发并使用，以支持临床医生、HSCPs和医疗管理人员在新成立的社区医疗网络中对信息需求的服务理解。确定的参与者具有跨学科性，包括职业治疗、物理治疗、言语和语言治疗以及营养学，同时还涉及公共卫生护理、全科医疗、健康和福祉以及初级保健管理（工作坊1：24人；工作坊2：18人）。

引导团队（6人）也是跨学科的，包括两名来自产品设计和设计工程学科的经验丰富的co-design引导者、两名HSCPs和两名医疗管理者（包括CHN经理）。引导团队有目的地设计、规划和准备了工作坊的结构和原始内容，以直接针对HSCPs的需求进行定制。Co-design工作坊按照COVID-19指南以面对面方式进行和引导。

Co-design采用设计主导的定性解释主义方法与利益相关者互动，并采用扎根理论方法进行分析。解释主义方法用于确保与参与者的共同理解和意义。由于其过程的包容性，以实践为基础的参与者主导的co-design是所使用的核心方法论。这确保了利益相关者不仅从自己的角度进行设计，还能分享和借鉴他人的经验，从而获得深入、共情且无偏见的理解。

对co-design经验的反思由设计和引导团队使用Gibbs反思循环进行。在此过程中，引导团队参与了协作反思会议，分享他们的观察和见解。这在每次工作坊后进行。描述和评估体验中的积极和消极感受与想法，分析以理解情况，并就所学内容以及未来可以做的不同之处得出结论。

经与国家HSE研发办公室咨询后，指导意见为：鉴于这是一项聚焦co-design方法论的服务质量改进举措，被视为不属于HSE RGMS框架范围。质量改进属于4.1.4节"不需要研究伦理委员会（REC）批准的活动"。

参与没有强制性，但鼓励对服务改进有兴趣且有时间/能力参加的人员参与，未提供参与激励。网络管理层鼓励参与，高级医疗管理层作为服务改进方法的一部分予以支持。工作坊期间准许参与者离开其临床角色。

引导co-design工作坊时，对学科和参与者之间任何潜在权力动态的认识非常重要。通过尽可能注意每组每位参与者的平等时间和发言机会来促进这一点。设计和引导团队在两次活动的介绍性展示中阐明了工作坊的目标，并强调了每个人跨学科贡献的重要性，以及需要确保聚焦于理解所有代表的信息需求，以迈向CHN中的整合团队方法。

参与者在co-design活动期间未在想法上记录姓名，强化了所有想法不论资历或学科均受到平等重视的信息。此外，更有意招募了资历较浅的HSCP代表以及学科管理人员，以获取多样化的信息需求，从而确保各职级员工的均衡参与。

Limitations · 局限性
English

Interim Analysis Phase · 中期分析阶段
English

An interim analysis phase was conducted by the design and facilitation team to organize identified themes and formulate a current position understanding from workshop 1. Workshop data were analyzed through a grounded theory approach. This was a process of data management and coding.

Coding is the "sensemaking" stage where analytic questions will be asked of the data, categorizing segments with a short name (a code) and using these codes to develop a deep understanding of what is happening. Approximately 450 sticky notes were coded into 50 themes in the first pass of coding. The second pass at coding synthesized these into 3 main themes with 12 sub-themes. Analysis and themes from co-design workshops were initially reported back to participants in workshop 2, with complete workshop findings presented at HSCP group presentations.

Discussion: 4 Key Learning Points on Co-Designing with Integrated Care Teams · 讨论：与整合护理团队Co-design的4条关键学习点
English

A specific objective of this paper is to reflect on the experience of co-designing used within integrated care to inform the 'how to do' of future practice. The following is a reflection synthesized into 4 key points, conducted using the Gibbs reflective cycle.

1. Preparation, Pre Planning and Planning for post-workshop analysis are key:

Pre-planning and tailoring of workshops prior to running and implementation are most important. 'Trial runs' of the process before workshops and prototyping of workshop assets to see how they may be interpreted by participants were key. In the planning stage, workshop assets, workflow, and timings should be revised to anticipate and mitigate potential adverse responses. This can help in mitigating potential challenges e.g., power and dynamics of participants, energy, and diversity of voice within participants.

Plan and prepare to make the best use of time. Professionals with high workloads need to feel workshops are fully considered and that their time is respected and well spent.

It is also important to allow for flexibility, iteration, and refinement as co-design workshops progress. Also, to allow for an improvement/learning cycle between workshops.

Plan for analysis and synthesis. This can be a labour-intensive and time-consuming activity to ensure rigour, especially manual coding. Factor in sufficient time for this activity and resources when applying for funding to conduct co-design.

2. Ensure Simplicity in Format:

The process of co-designing in groups benefits from being visual and designing simple inclusive formats with easy-to-interpret assets. From the perspective of facilitation, simplification of assets to record workshops e.g., the use of simplified posters and specifically created personas were key for engagement. This allows for time efficiency in facilitation and elicits quick and diverse inputs from participants.

3. Interdisciplinary Co-design and Co-facilitation of Workshops:

The design and facilitation team consisted of design practitioners together with subject matter experts, healthcare managers and clinicians. Having an interdisciplinary team offers benefits both in terms of designing appropriate workshops and assets and the ability for healthcare practitioners to replicate the activity for future studies, independent of the support of the design practitioners.

Input of integrated care team managers is critical to ensure that findings from co-design processes are honoured and implemented where possible. This supports staff in their transitioning to new integrated team structures and allows an understanding of their value within the service they provide while simultaneously delivering on the quadruple aim of Sláintecare.

4. Co-designing can enhance communication for service improvement:

Facilitators felt that the co-design workshops helped in enhancing interdisciplinary communication. The act of getting a diversity of healthcare staff including HSCPs and their Public Health Nursing and GP colleagues in a room together to discuss integrated care was considered powerful and important in itself.

The design and facilitation team felt that co-design provided a means for the workforce to identify and develop different ways of working together, to sustain this practice and improve. The workshops were seen to identify better ways of working to support integrated care, identifying a continuum of activities to build integrated care capacity.

1. 准备、预规划和工后分析规划是关键：

为分析和综合做好规划。这可能是一项劳动密集型和耗时的活动以确保严谨性，尤其是手动编码。在申请co-design资金时，需要为这项活动和资源预留充足时间。

2. 确保形式简洁：

小组co-design过程受益于视觉化和设计简单包容的格式及易于解读的资产。从引导角度来看，简化记录工作坊的资产（如使用简化海报和专门创建的人物角色）是参与的关键。这提高了引导的时间效率，并能从参与者那里快速获得多样化的输入。

设计和引导团队由设计实践者与主题专家、医疗管理者和临床医生共同组成。跨学科团队在设计适当工作坊和资产方面具有优势，同时也使医疗从业者能够在没有设计实践者支持的情况下独立复制活动供未来研究使用。

整合护理团队管理者的参与对于确保co-design过程的发现得到尊重和在可能时实施至关重要。这支持员工向新整合团队结构的过渡，并使他们理解自己在所提供的服务中的价值，同时实现Sláintecare的四重目标。

4. Co-design能够增强服务改进中的沟通：

引导者认为co-design工作坊有助于增强跨学科沟通。将多元化的医疗工作人员（包括HSCPs及其公共卫生护士和全科医生同事）聚集在一个房间里讨论整合护理，这一行为本身即被视为强有力且重要的。

设计和引导团队认为，co-design为工作人员识别和发展不同协作方式、持续改进实践提供了手段。这些工作坊被视为识别更好的工作方式以支持整合护理、构建整合护理能力连续体的途径。

Conclusion · 结论
English

This paper aimed to inform future practice in the understanding of the development, use and facilitation of co-design with integrated care teams. Conducted through a process of quality improvement, we sought to establish a shared understanding of information needs among a newly formed integrated care team. We developed and used co-design methods to understand the 'current and future position' of service information needs from the team perspective to support good practice in integrated care provision.

The workshops offered a wide range of service information needs. Culture Building, System Needs, and Processes were identified as key themes in future information needs for healthcare staff working in CHNs. Offering a reflection of methods used, we conclude with 4 key learning points on co-designing with Integrated Care Teams: (1) Ensure simplicity in format; (2) Interdisciplinary co-design and co-facilitation of workshops are beneficial; (3) Planning and preparation of workshops are key; (4) Co-designing can enhance communication for service improvement.

Future co-design work will now be progressed to help develop these findings and evolve best practices in integrated care service improvement. This future work will focus on the information needs of both service staff and users ensuring equality and diversity of voice of all participants.

Key Takeaways · 核心要点
English
Finding 1

Co-design bridges information gaps in integrated care. Two co-design workshops with 24 and 18 interdisciplinary participants identified culture, systems, and processes as the three key themes for future information needs.

Finding 2

Design-led methodology enables shared understanding. Using personas, affinity diagramming, and timeline canvases, the team enabled HSCPs from diverse disciplines to articulate and align their information needs.

Finding 3

Preparation and simplicity are critical success factors. Trial runs, prototyping of workshop assets, and simplified formats (posters, personas) were essential for engagement with busy healthcare professionals.

Finding 4

Interdisciplinary co-facilitation is beneficial. A facilitation team of design practitioners, HSCPs, and healthcare managers enabled both rigorous workshop design and future replicability by healthcare staff.

Finding 5

Co-design enhances cross-disciplinary communication. Bringing diverse healthcare staff together to discuss integrated care was powerful in itself, identifying a continuum of activities to build integrated care capacity.

Finding 6

Future work must include patients and service users. The authors acknowledge that true co-design requires equal inclusion of patient/service user voices to ensure equality and diversity of perspectives.
